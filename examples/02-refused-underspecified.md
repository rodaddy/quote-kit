# Worked example 2 — the kit says no

**This is the most important example in the folder.** It is the run where
nothing gets produced.

A quoting tool that always gives you a number is a form-filler. The expensive
mistake in this trade is not a typo in the arithmetic — it is a firm price on a
job nobody measured. So the kit has one condition under which it stops flat:

> **Never quote an underspecified job. An unpriceable quote is worse than no
> quote.**

**Business:** Vitale Landscaping and Masonry (same invented business as example
1, same filled rate card).

---

## What went in

The owner typed one line, on his phone, between jobs:

> quote the Hendricks job, drainage work out back, call it $3,800

That looks quotable. It has a customer, a job, and a price. It is not.

---

## What came out: no quote, no number

> I can't quote this yet — here's what's missing:
>
> 1. **What the drainage work actually is.** Your services file lists three
>    different drainage jobs at three different prices: a dry well (flat
>    $1,850), a French drain (per foot), and regrading (per square foot). I
>    can't write a line item until I know which one, and a French drain also
>    needs the run in feet.
> 2. **Where the water goes.** Your file says you need the discharge point
>    before you price any drainage job — daylight, dry well, or street.
> 3. **Where the $3,800 came from.** If that's a number you worked out on site,
>    tell me and I'll use it as a flat price with the scope written out. If it's
>    a ballpark, I'd rather price it off your rate card than put a guess in
>    front of a customer with your name on top of it.
>
> Give me those and I'll price it and give it a number.

**Nothing else happened.** Specifically:

- **No quote number was assigned.** The ledger still ends at 0044.
- **No ledger row was written.** There is no half-quote sitting in the record.
- **No subtotal or total was shown**, not even "so you can see it."
- **No price was invented** to fill any of the three gaps.

The kit will hold there. If the owner says he has to go measure, the answer is
"fine — measure it and come back, this stays un-numbered until then."

---

## The one exception, and how it is written

The owner CAN quote a flat price he worked out himself. He just has to say so,
and then the scope goes in writing:

> Owner: it's a French drain, about 60 feet, out to daylight at the property
> line. The $3,800 is what I figured on site.
>
> Kit: got it — flat price with the scope spelled out, since it's your number
> rather than the per-foot rate. That reads as "French drain, approximately 60
> feet, discharging to daylight at the rear property line - installed." One
> job, $3,800.00. Exclusions from your list: permit, utility marking, lawn
> restoration over the trench, and anything we hit in the trench run. It
> assumes the 60 feet holds when we lay it out and that daylight discharge is
> legal at that property line. Want me to number it?

That is the difference between a number the owner chose and a number the tool
made up. The kit will carry the first one all day. It will never produce the
second.

---

## What to notice

1. **A stated price does not excuse the rest of the check.** "$3,800" satisfied
   nothing. Scope, quantity, and the pricing questions still had to be answered.
2. **The refusal names what it CAN see.** It knew the customer, it knew the
   category of work, it knew the rate card had three candidates. It said all of
   that, then asked the one question per gap that unblocks it.
3. **The ledger stayed clean.** Numbers are only spent on quotes that were
   actually priceable. That is why the sequence never has holes in it and why
   "what's still open?" is a number the owner can trust.
